# The town, the routes and the way to school

This is Kamakura's town, the original. The game's five cities, each with its own home, school and routes, are designed in
`docs/CITIES.md`; Kamakura's new routes there will replace the six below once built.

One home, one school, six ways between them. **Every route is one trip from the front door to the school gate, and each story level is one leg of it**: the route's first level starts on the home front, its last level ends at the school's arch, and every level in between runs from one stop on the way to the next (the bus stop, granny's house, the church, the station...). The level that ends at a stop and the next one on the same route, which starts there, build the same stop, so the legs join up. The map shows the town with the six routes drawn from the house to the school, every stop at its own place in the town (the station at the foot of the railway bridge, the surf school on the boardwalk where the beach begins...), and each level's node on its own leg near where the level starts: a route's first node just past the streets it shares with other routes outside the house, every later node shortly after the stop it starts from.

Nothing here has been run in Unity or on a device (see PROGRESS.md for what was checked offline).

Only the first level of a route starts at home, and only the last ends at the school. Chapter 4's levels alternate between its two routes (L15 underpass, L16 and L17 site, L18 underpass), so L18 picks up the underpass trip at the station where L15 left it. Each stop has its name in both languages (`stop.<id>`) and a phrase for "Made it to..." (`stop.<id>.to`: "the bakery", "a la panadería"); the levels' signs and boards use its English `signName`, as all the levels' signs are English. `RouteTableTests` checks the chain (home, the stops in order, the school; each stop on one route; names in both languages; a set piece for each).

Chapters stay the unit of the paywall (`Store/Monetization.IsChapterUnlocked`), of the Holiday Run's stages and of the level catalog; routes are how the map and the levels present them. Chapter 4 has two routes, and its levels alternate between them (15 underpass, 16 and 17 site, 18 underpass). A story level unlocks when the one before it in the catalog is done and, if that one ended a route at the school, when that route's trip has got to school on time once (`Progression/LevelUnlocks`, "School trips" below); a challenge unlocks with its chapter's first level.

## School trips (`Progression/SchoolTrips.cs`, `Core/LevelManager.cs`, `UI/GameHud.cs`)

A route is a stage, and the stage is one trip to school: the times of its legs add up, and the school bell is for the whole trip.

- **Each leg has its own target**, its `LevelDefinition.bellTime` (the street's seconds plus the 3 s walk from the stop, as before). It is a pace, not a bell: the HUD shows "Target 0:28" counting down (orange under 10 s, "Behind" once past it, never "LATE!"), and keeping it earns the leg's second star.
- **The school bell of a trip** is the sum of its legs' targets (`SchoolTrips.TripBell`, looked up in the catalog):

  | Route | Legs | The bell |
  |---|---|---|
  | Home Street | L01 to L05: 53 + 58 + 83 + 63 + 83 s | 5:40 |
  | Market Street | L06 to L10: 63 + 73 + 73 + 78 + 98 s | 6:25 |
  | Seaside Road | L11 to L14: 73 + 78 + 83 + 98 s | 5:32 |
  | The Underpass | L15, L18: 68 + 108 s | 2:56 |
  | Building Site Shortcut | L16, L17: 83 + 88 s | 2:51 |
  | Park Path | L19 to L23: 68 + 78 + 83 + 83 + 123 s | 7:15 |

- **The trip is saved** per route (`SaveData.trips`, a `TripRecord`: `routeId`, `legTimes` with -1 for a leg not walked in this trip, `onTime`, `bestTripTime`). Finishing a route's first leg starts a new trip (every leg forgotten, leg 0 set). Finishing a later leg sets its time and forgets the legs after it: a trip is one walk, in order, though a leg can be walked again before going on. The HUD adds the trip so far to the leg line on a later leg ("Home » Bakery · Trip 2:15", live), and a stop's results say "Trip so far 2:15 · school bell at 6:25" (green while the trip is within the legs' targets so far).
- **Only a route's last leg has the bell**, and only when every leg before it was walked in this trip: the HUD shows "Bell 0:28" for what is left of the whole trip, and it rings ("LATE!") when the trip so far plus this leg's time reaches the bell. When it rings, the trip is forgotten at once (`SchoolTrips.MissBell`), so restarting the last leg cannot save it.
- **At school:** on time (at or before the bell) the results say "Made it to school on time!" and "Whole trip before the bell (4:58 of 5:40)", the route is marked `onTime` for good and its best trip kept. Late, they say "Late for school!", the whole trip is forgotten, a red note says to walk the whole way again from home to unlock the next level, the main button becomes **From home** (the route's first leg) and Next is hidden while the next level is locked.
- **The last leg on its own** (played from the map, or after the trip was broken) is untimed: no bell, its own target instead, "Made it to school!" and a note to walk the whole way from home to time the trip. It never opens the next stage.
- **Unlocking:** a story level needs the story level before it in the catalog done and, if that level is the last leg of its route, that route's trip `onTime` (`LevelUnlocks.StoryOpen`). In chapter 4, L18 needs the site's trip (L16, L17) on time and L19 the underpass's (L15, then L18; the site's legs walked in between do not touch it). A level already finished stays open. When the first level that cannot be played is held back by a late trip, `LevelUnlocks.NextToPlay` returns that route's first leg, so the map focuses the stage to walk again. Challenges keep their rule.
- **Next** after a leg goes to the next leg of the same route when it can be played (after L15, L18 once it is open), else to the next story level of the catalog.
- **Stars:** the second star is the pace on a stop's leg or an untimed last leg, and the whole trip before the bell on a timed last leg; the third still needs the second.
- **Cozy mode** (the bell turned off in the settings): no bell, no target, the trip is not recorded or judged ("Cozy mode: no bell" in the results), and stages open without an on-time trip. Switching it in the pause menu takes effect on resuming.
- **Old saves:** `SaveData.CurrentVersion` is 2. `SaveSystem.Migrate` calls `SchoolTrips.Upgrade`, which marks every route whose last level a version 1 save had finished as on time, so nobody finds a level locked that they could play before.
- **Tests:** `SchoolTripTests` (the trip rules, the unlock rule with chapter 4's interleaving, `NextToPlay`, the upgrade; plain data, no Unity), `RouteTableTests.TheTripTestsWalkTheStoryInTheLevelTablesOrder` and `EveryRouteHasASchoolBellForTheWholeTrip`, `GameLogicTests.TripsSurviveTheSaveFile` (JsonUtility).

The town as rendered (`tools/blender/town_map.py`, see "The picture" below; positions in picture pixels):

- **Home** (820, 1045) in the west of the core, where West Street meets Elm Street: the kid's red-door house (the Buildings kit's HouseGable, as in every level) on its corner with the front garden, in a neighbourhood of small houses on an orderly grid of rounded blocks. All six routes start there.
- **The school** (2385, 825) in the north-east by the sea: the building, its yard and a football pitch with a running track, the grounds' east fence on the sand.
- **The beach and the sea** along the east: sand from the school down to the south-east, a boardwalk, parasols, palms, a rescue-board stand, boats, tidal channels, slalom flags (C03). The beach route (Seaside Road) runs along the south of the town, onto the sand and up the shore to the school.
- **The park** across the north: lawns and trees, its path with three fountains, trampolines, a playground with swings over a stream, a duck pond, a lawn arena (C05). The park route goes up Park Road, along the path, through a short tunnel under the railway (L23) and down to the school.
- **The railway** comes in from the north edge and ends at the station in the middle, just south of Station Road, so no road crosses it on the level. The underpass route runs along Station Road, past the building site, to the station's brick ticket hall at the foot of the railway bridge (the stop between L15 and L18), then in a cutting under two bridge decks (the main line and the station's loop).
- **The building site** fills the middle blocks between Station Road and Elm Street: two fenced yards with cranes, trenches, pipes and cabins, with the site track (the site route, in from Yard Road) through them and the site office at a security gate halfway along it. No railway on it. Along Station Road its covered walkways shelter the footpath (L15's dark stretches).
- **The stops** stand where the picture shows them: the bus stop's shelter, granny's pink bungalow, St Mary's church with its tower and spire at the top of Hill Road and Sam's yellow house along Elm Street; the bakery, the post office, the flower shop and the library on Market Street's south side; the surf school's hut where the boardwalk leaves Seaside Road for the sand, the lifeguard tower and the beach cafe's kiosk; the station's hall; the site office's cabin; the bandstand, the park cafe, the boathouse at the duck pond's end and grandpa's allotment by the railway in the park.
- **The market** in the south-middle along Market Street: shops with awnings built to the pavement, a market plaza with stalls, zebra crossings, traffic; C02's block of shopfronts on Mill Street at its west end.
- **Home Street's route** is the direct way: Elm Street, Hill Road and Garden Lane east from the house, then Puddle Lane up to the school.
- Streets are realistic: the routes follow them (and the park's path) and meet only at their ends, sharing a short stem near home (West Street, Elm Street) and near the school (East Street, School Lane). The picture carries no text of its own and no route lines, nodes or markers: the game draws those.

Rules that follow from the levels, and how each side keeps them:

- **The railway.** Only the routes whose levels have a railway tunnel cross the railway, and they pass under it: the underpass route (twice, both in L18) and the park route (once, at L23). No other route crosses the line at all, not even by a level crossing, because their levels have none: the picture ends the line at the station or routes it so (the art's own check counts each route's crossings). In the levels every tunnel is that railway's bridge (`TrackBuilder.Tunnel`): a band in the red of the map's train along the front of the deck and, every 8 m or so, a white plate with that red train on its rails. The signs say so: L18 "Tunnel Run! Twice under the railway", and L23 has "Under the railway - nearly at school!" just before its tunnel. **L15 reaches the station only at its end**, so its two dark stretches are not the railway but the building site's covered walkways on Station Road (`TrackBuilder.CoveredWalkway`: the same roof, collider, lamps and darkness as a tunnel, with the site's hoarding along the back, a boarded roof over the footpath, the site's blue boards with hazard stripes along its front and scaffold tubes); its sign says "Covered walkway - it's dark in there!".
- **The stops are where the levels say.** The station's hall is on Station Road at the foot of the bridge, so the level that ends there (L15) has no railway and the one that starts there (L18) goes under it at once; the surf school is on the boardwalk where the beach begins (L11 ends with its rack); the site office is inside the site, so both site levels are building-site levels; grandpa's allotment is by the railway, so L23 starts under it.
- **The school** is by the sea (L23's gulls; the beach route ends there) with its own pitch.
- **The building site's shortcut** has no tunnel, so it does not go under the railway.
- **Street names.** Every level's route sign reads "School ->, via <route name>" (in English, like all the levels' signs). On the map the road each street-like route follows carries that name: "Home Street" on the street route, "Market Street" on the market route, "Seaside Road" on the beach route. The street route's places are named beside their nodes (Elm Street, Hill Road, Garden Lane, Puddle Lane), and no street label is the name of a level other than Puddle Lane.

`TownMapTests.TheStreetsTheRoutesFollowAreNamedAsTheLevelsSignsNameThem` checks the street names on the real layout (each of the three names is written within 60 px of its route). `TownMapTests.EveryStopIsDrawnWhereTheLayoutPlacesIt` and `EveryLevelsNodeIsOnItsOwnLegNearWhereItStarts` check the stops and the legs (below). The art's own validator also checks the railway crossings, that each story node sits in its level's area (`LEVEL_ZONE`: L15 on the way to the station, L18 in the underpass, L11 on the way down to the sea, L16 on Yard Road, L17 in the site, L23 past the park's tunnel...) and each stop in its own (`STOP_ZONE`: the station at the foot of the bridge, the surf school at the start of the beach, the site office in the site, the allotment by the railway), since the JSON does not hold the railway or the areas.

A stop's point is where the picture shows the place (the bus stop's shelter, the station's hall...), on its route; the screen draws the stop's marker there (`TownMap.StopPosition`, which falls back to halfway between the two levels' nodes for a layout without stops). `label` is the side of its node on which the level's name is preferably written (the screen moves it where it fits, see "The map screen"); `angle` is the street name's rotation in degrees, clockwise on screen (y down). A node is a 150 px disc. District names are the strings `district.<id>` (English and Spanish); street names are shown as given, never translated.

`Tests/Editor/TownMapTests.cs` checks the file against the picture and `RouteTable`: the frame and core are as agreed and the picture is 3200 x 1800; every story level and challenge has exactly one node, each with a name side (below, above, left or right); nodes and places lie inside the core's inner area (core-relative x 120..2280, y 170..1130); node discs are 190 px or more apart; a story node is within 12 px of its own route and the nodes follow the playing order along it; every stop has its place in the layout, within 2 px of its own route, and is drawn there, between its two levels' nodes and clear of every node; every story node lies on its own leg (between the stop or home it starts at and the stop or school it ends at), a route's first node past the stem it shares with other routes near home (where the route is 75 px from every other route) and at most 500 px along beyond it, every later node at most 260 px along its route after its stop; a challenge node is 130 to 260 px from its route; the routes are RouteTable's six in its order, start at home and end at the school (within 25 px), are 1200 px or longer, and keep 90 px apart except within 320 px of the two ends; for every route in focus and every language, every level name finds a spot clear of its route's nodes and stop markers, the pins, the place names and the other names (`TownMapNames.Place`; at most four lie over another route's smaller node), and the name widths follow the label font; Home Street, Market Street and Seaside Road are written along their routes (see "Level and map"); the six route colours tell apart; every string the map uses exists in both languages.

### The picture

`tools/blender/town_map.py` builds the town from the art kits (the same houses, shops, school, trees, cars, site machinery and beach props as the levels) and renders it top-down in Blender (Eevee), with the layout file written from the same plan: `venv_bpy/bin/python tools/blender/town_map.py -- <out dir> [--debug]` (about 5.5 minutes; deterministic). `tools/blender/town_map_check.py` validates an installed layout against `RouteTable.cs` and `StringTables.cs`: the contract above, the name rules, the street names, the trip (the stops on their routes between their levels, every node on its own leg near its start), and what the JSON cannot hold (each story node in its level's area and each stop in its own, and each route's railway crossings: street 0, market 0, beach 0, underpass 2, site 0, park 1, always under the line). `tools/blender/README.md` ("The town map", "Level and map") describes the generator and lists what the picture shows at each of the 28 nodes. A layout marked `"placeholder": true` (the stand-in this replaced) skips the names' room and street-name tests.

## The map screen (`FrontEnd/ChapterMapScreen.cs`, data in `Levels/TownMap.cs`)

- **The picture fills the screen and pans and zooms** (`UI/Kit/UIMapView`, its arithmetic in `UI/Kit/MapViewMath`). It is a `RawImage` in a 3200 x 1800 block inside a view the size of the whole screen; the header and the bars at the bottom lie over it. At zoom 1 (the fit) the part that matters, every node with its stars, both pins and every route with a 40 px margin (about 2355 x 1231 px), fits the room between the header and the strip and left of the zoom buttons, up to 5 % bigger if that makes the picture cover the screen to its edges: 0.6 canvas units per picture pixel at 16:9 (was 0.48 with the old title row and the core fitted), 0.58 at 4:3. **One finger** (or the mouse) drags it, **two fingers** pinch-zoom about their middle, the **mouse wheel** zooms about the pointer, and the **+ / - / fit buttons** at the right glide in, out (x1.6) and back to the fit. It zooms from the fit to 3x (never past 1.6 canvas units a pixel, where the picture would go soft), a fling drifts to a stop, and the picture never slides off a side of the screen it covers. A drag that starts on a node moves the map and does not play the level; a tap plays it. Zoomed in, choosing another route glides to its levels. Opening the map fits it again.
- **Things on the picture keep a size that suits the zoom** (`MapViewMath.ItemScale`): nodes, pins, names, district and street names and the route lines grow on screen with the zoom to the power 0.4 rather than with the picture (at 3x they are about 1.55x as big on screen but a third of their old size against the town), each scaled about the point it belongs to, so they get bigger and further apart, easier to read and tap, without covering the town. The route lines and pins are drawn again at the new widths when the zoom has changed by more than 2 %.
- **The stops** between the levels are small white markers with a dot of the route's colour on the line, where the layout places each one (in front of the bus stop's shelter, the station's hall, the surf school's hut...; `TownMap.StopPosition` puts the layout's point exactly on the line, and falls back to halfway between the two levels' nodes for a layout without stops; `TownMapTests.EveryStopIsDrawnWhereTheLayoutPlacesIt` and `StopMarkersSitOnTheirRouteBetweenTheirLevelsClearOfEveryNode` check them). The focused route's level names keep off its stop markers (`TownMapNames.Place`). The focused route's stops are named once the view is zoomed in (1.3x or more) and the name fits beside its marker, on the side of the line with less in the way, touching no node, stars pill, level name, pin, place name, district or street name or another stop's name at the current scale (`UpdateStopLabels`).
- The header's title is the map's prompt, "Choose your way to school" (it shrinks to fit between the back button and the wallet); the old title row under the header, with the focused route's card, is gone, since the route chips show the same. If the picture is missing, a plain background takes its place and everything else still works; if the layout is missing, the picture shows with only the strip and the chips to play from.
- **On the picture** the game draws, kept clean so the route in focus reads at a glance: a light, even **wash** over the picture (`PictureWash`, white at 24 %); the **focused route** as a coloured line 54 px wide with a white casing and a dashed centre from home to the school (chapter 4's two routes are two shades of its colour), its stops, and a dotted branch to its challenge; the **other routes** as thin, faint lines in their colours (17 px, half see-through, no casing, stops or branches: a chip at the bottom brings each into focus); a red **house** pin and a blue **school** pin with their names; each of the focused route's levels as a **numbered node** (a 150 px disc in the route's colour, the stars on a white pill on its lower rim, the next level to play pulsing with a soft halo; locked, a lock and no stars on a pale shade of the route's colour) and its challenge as a gold **"!"** node; of the other routes' nodes only the ones the map has to point out (`Drawn`: the next level to play and one a late trip holds back, at 70 % and without stars, with their PLAY or LATE tags); the focused route's **level names** (white with a dark outline, 50 px of the picture) where they fit; the focused route's own **street name** (Home Street, Market Street, Seaside Road); and, once the view is zoomed in to 1.4x or more (`DetailZoom`), the **district names** (small capitals) and the other **street names** (small, along the road at the angle given), each only where it touches no node, name, pin or route line. There is no "You are here" pill: the next level's PLAY tag says where to go, and the house pin has its name.
- **Where the names go** (`Levels/TownMapNames.cs`, rebuilt each time the focus moves): first the house's and the school's names beside their pins (`PlaceBox`: the first of six spots round the pin clear of every node, furthest from the route lines), then the focused route's level names (`Place`): each beside its node on the layout's side if it fits, else on one of eight spots round it (the four sides, slid a little along, and the four corners), on two lines if that helps (broken at the space that balances the lines, so that no line is wider than 380, then 300, then 200 px) and at 46 px as a last resort. A name never covers its own route's nodes and stars, the pins, the place names or another name (and keeps 30 px from other names where it can); it keeps off the other routes' (smaller) nodes where it can. Sizes come from the label font's advances (Lilita One at 50 px, a table in the code, measured from the font file), so the screen and the tests agree. Then the district names and the street names take what room is left (the hidden nodes of the other routes leave theirs free). The school district's name is left out: its pin already says School.
- **Along the bottom:** the focused route's levels as a strip like a level select (a **LEVELS** tag, a house, a chip for each level with its number or a lock and its stars, a "!" chip for the challenge, the school, and a marker over the next level) and under it a **chip for each of the six routes** (colour, name, stars out of the total; once the route has been walked all the way to school, its medal hangs over the chip's top right corner: `Cosmetics/RouteRewards`) that moves the focus. Level chips and nodes play the level (`Controller.PlayLevel`); locked ones do nothing.
- The route with the next level to play (`LevelUnlocks.NextToPlay`), or the one asked for through `FrontEndController.RequestedChapter` (the route of that chapter that holds the next level, else its first), starts in focus.
- Paywalled chapters (`Monetization.IsChapterUnlocked`) keep today's behaviour: their levels are locked and an unlock button (`UnlockPanel`) sits on the focused route.
- Touch targets: a node's tap area is a clear box at least 118 canvas units across at the fit (more than its 90 unit disc) and grows with the node as the view zooms in; a strip slot 100 x 104, a route chip about 120 x 110, a zoom button 108 across. Text is never under 22 canvas units (names 24; a route chip's name shrinks to 22 and takes a second line on a narrow screen); every string goes through `Loc` in English and Spanish.
- The picture is loaded when the map is shown and released (`Resources.UnloadAsset`) when it is hidden or the screen is rebuilt. `Editor/UiArtImportSettings` imports it as an opaque texture (up to 4096, no mipmaps, bilinear, clamped, kept at 3200 x 1800, high-quality compression), not as a sprite like the other pictures in `Resources/UI`.

## Every route is one trip, a level is a leg of it (`Levels/RouteTable.cs`, `Editor/LevelBuildKit.Home.cs`, `LevelBuildKit.Stops.cs`, `StreetBuilder.Home.cs`, `StreetBuilder.Stops.cs`, `HomeLeadInRules.cs`, `Levels/HomeInfo.cs`)

**Nothing here has been seen in Unity** (see PROGRESS.md for what was checked offline and what was not).

- **Where a level starts and ends.** `BuildScene` hands the track builder the level's `From` and `To` stops (`RouteTable.From` / `To`). `Start` builds the start: the home front for the first level of a route (below), else the stop the level before ended at (`BeginAtStop`). `Goal` builds the end: the school's arch for the last level of a route, else the stop the next level starts at (`StopGoal`): the stop's set piece with its door at the layout's goal x, a chalk line across the footpath just past it, and the goal zone from there on (the kid and the ball both have to cross it). Layouts are unchanged: each still calls `Start` and `Goal` once, at the same x, so the feasibility audit says what it said.
- **The stops' set pieces** (`StreetBuilder.StopFront`, one per stop, deterministic, the same at both ends of the two levels that share it) are built from the art kits, with the people who belong there (`TrackBuilder.StopPeople`: the pedestrians' adult and the younger kid's model in their rest pose):
  - *Bus stop:* a glass-backed shelter with a bench, BUS STOP on its fascia and a timetable, the stop's round sign on its pole, a bin, a hedge; someone waiting.
  - *Granny's house:* a pastel bungalow behind a white picket fence, flower beds and roses, a bench, her ginger cat on the gatepost and a little sign; granny at the gate.
  - *Church:* St Mary's, a stone church with its gable to the street, an arched door, a round window and tall windows, a square tower with a clock (ten to eight), a belfry, a slate spire (`World/SpireShape`) and a cross; the churchyard wall and gateway, a path, conifers, a bench and the notice board.
  - *Sam's house:* a bright flat-roofed house, bikes and a ball on the lawn, number 12; Sam at the gate with a backpack.
  - *Shops* (the bakery, the post office, the flower shop, the library, the park cafe): a Buildings-kit shop painted for it with its name on a board over the door, and its things out front (a chalkboard, benches, planters, a post box, a bike rack, buckets of flowers, cafe tables).
  - *On the beach:* the surf school's hut with its board rack, towels and flag; the lifeguard tower with its rescue boards, its red-and-yellow flags and a lifeguard; the beach cafe's kiosk with tables and parasols. All stand on the dry sand, clear of the water (the kit's shops are 8 m deep and would reach into the sea, so the cafe is a kiosk).
  - *Station:* the railway station's brick ticket hall, STATION over its wide doorway, a clock, the railway's red band; a traveller.
  - *Site office:* the cabin behind the site fence, the sliding gate run back, a portable loo, a cement mixer, a wheelbarrow; a builder in a hard hat.
  - *In the park:* the bandstand with benches and flower beds; the boathouse on the duck pond with a raft and reeds; grandpa's allotment with a green shed, rows of vegetables and a wheelbarrow, grandpa among them.

  Each stop's plot (12 m round its door, 15 m for the church) is a quiet area: the street's own lots and poles, and the beach's parasols, towers and palms, keep out of it. The set pieces are built last in `Finish`, once the ground (and on the beach the sand) is known.
- **The route sign** before the level's own street says where the level goes and by which route: "School ->" on the last level of a route, else the stop's name ("Granny's house ->", "Station ->"), then "via <route name>".
- **The HUD and the results** say the leg: under the level's name, "Granny's house » Church" (`leg.fromTo`); a level that ends at a stop says "Made it to the church!" and "Reached the church with the ball" (`results.madeTo`, `results.reachedTo` with `stop.<id>.to`); the last level of a route keeps "Made it to school!".
- **A lead-in of 12 m** (`LevelBuildKit.HomeLeadIn`, from `HomeLeadInRules.Length`) in front of every story level (L01 to L23), the same length wherever it starts, so every bell grows by the same 3 s. A level that starts at a stop walks 12 m of its own route's street from the stop's door (no seam). The first level of a route starts on the doorstep of the house with the ball, walks the lead-in, and the route's own street begins where the layout's own x = 0 always was.
- **The bell grows by the walk:** `GeneratedContent.Bell(seconds)` adds `HomeLeadInRules.BellSeconds` (12 m at 4.2 m/s is 2.9 s, rounded up to 3) to each story level's authored street seconds, so the star thresholds mean what they did.
- **A first level's lead-in looks like the home street** (chapter 1: lawns, houses, garden walls, poles, the footpath, road and verge of chapter 1) whatever the route. A chapter-1 level is one street, no seam. In the other four chapters the track has a **seam** at the layout's start (`TrackBuilder.SeamX`, on the sand's half-unit lattice):
  - *Ground:* the home street's slabs (road, footpath, verge; `LevelBuildKit.AsChapter(1, ...)` sets the static `Chapter` and `Theme` and puts them back) run from the world's start to the seam and are closed there; the route's own slabs (or, on the beach, its sand: `BeachGround` starts exactly at the seam) begin at the seam and are closed on that side, as the Holiday Run's stages are where chapters change (their surfaces stand at slightly different heights; each cap shows only as a step).
  - *Scenery:* two passes of the Holiday Run's stage scenery (`StreetBuilder.SceneryWithSeam`): the route's own chapter from the seam to 50 m past the level's end, built first, with the distant land too (`StageSpec.level`: fields and hills, the sea and its headlands, the site's cranes, because a level has no core scene), and chapter 1 from 50 m behind the back wall to the seam. Each pass builds exactly its stretch (lots, shops, poles, animals fit and stop at the seam; no back-row building or far row straddles it), the home pass's road gets ragged patches of the route's road surface and the route's pass patches of the home road, and on the beach a few props of the home street stand on the sand's edge (the stages' `SeamDressing`).
  - *At the seam itself:* a kerb stone across the footpath, a pair of bollards beside it, and **the route sign** 2.3 m before it.
- The route sign's route name is the English one (`RouteTable.Of(levelId).NameKey`; the levels' signs are English in every language, see `StringTables`). It is on chapter-1 levels too ("via Home Street").
- **The home front** (`TrackBuilder.HomeFront(x)` with `StreetBuilder.HomeFront`) is one set piece, the same at the start of every route (and of the Holiday Run's lap): the Buildings kit's **HouseGable** in `HomeInfo`'s colours (cream walls, a warm red front door, slate roof) with its door under the **gate** where the kid starts; a picket fence along the street, an open garden gate between two pillars (a pot on each, the house number 7 on one), hedges at the ends, a path to the doorstep, flower beds, a bush and pots, a bike on its stand, the bins, a post box on the footpath and a **parent** (the pedestrians' adult model, in its rest pose) on the top step. `HomeInfo` (house model, door colour, number) holds what the map needs to draw the same house, so the two match. The plot (`StreetBuilder.HomePlotLeft` to `HomePlotRight` around the gate) is a quiet area: the street's own lots keep out of it.
- **Tutorial:** L01's first lesson moved with the start (the "dribble" hint to x = -10.5, the walk sign to x = -8: 1.5 m and 4 m from the doorstep as they were from the old start). In every other level the first sign was 5 to 10 m from the start and is now 17 to 22 m from the door the level starts at (home's or a stop's), and the first hint (7 to 24 m before) is now 19 to 36 m: all of them come right after the lead-in, where they introduce the level's own street.
- The school end is the existing `TrackBuilder.Goal` arch, now in the last level of each route (all 23 story layouts call `Goal` exactly once; the audit checks it). **On the beach (L14) the school stands on land:** the coast turns away from the beach 26 m before the gate (`StreetBuilder.SchoolHeadland`): a low sea wall with rocks at its foot runs back diagonally from the water's edge to 46 m behind the footpath and on along the back, grass fills the land behind the beach's dry sand, the yard past the gate is paved from the camera's side to behind the footpath (the sand under it is calm, so no hummock pokes through), and the sea lies beyond the wall, so the school, its boundary wall and its trees all stand on solid ground with the sea behind them. The beach's parasols, towers, palms, rocks, foam and wave crests stop short of the school.
- Challenges (C01 to C05) are ball-skill arenas and keep their own start.
- The Holiday Run keeps its own world: all 23 stages in story order from the home street to the school gate. Its lap also starts at the kid's front door: stage 1 builds the same home front at the spawn point (`HolidayRunBuilder`, `TrackBuilder.HomeFront(HolidayStages.SpawnX)`).
