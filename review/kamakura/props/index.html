Kamakura props

KM1-1's street props, buildings, set pieces and the tram, each built in high definition from your own views of it (front, right side, back, top and three-quarter, made in ChatGPT). A prop is carved as one solid from its views' outlines. A building or set piece (the house, the shrine, the platform) is modelled from simple solids measured in metres off your views: walls, roofs, steps, posts and rails. Either way it is then textured from the views themselves: every point takes its pixels from the view that faces it squarely, can see it and shows the object there. Heights or widths are the real ones from the prompts; the rest comes from your views' proportions.

Approved and in the game: twenty-two of them are in the project (Assets/_Game/Art/Models/Kamakura). The delivery scooter (KM-V03) is in too, on your go-ahead: 1.8 m long, 2,910 triangles in a 1024 texture, its spokes and basket mesh cut out; KM1-1 parks it at a door while its rider delivers. A prop is about 1,450 triangles in a 1024 texture; the house 4,850, the shrine and the platform 3,880 each, the gantry 2,910 and the tram 3,878, each in a 2048 texture. The board puts each beside your views: your front view and the model straight on at the same scale, your three-quarter view and the model at three-quarters, then the model from the street.

New for KM1-4 (16 November): the big old ginkgo (KM-G12, 20 m, 2,678 triangles), the cherry in blossom (KM-G16, 7 m, 2,830 triangles; its thin trunk joined as a solid, D34) and the lotus (KM-G22, 1.2 m, 1,432 triangles), carved from your views like KM1-1's trees. Not installed yet: they go in with KM1-4.

New for KM2-1 (16 November): the dune grass with its beach shrub (KM-G23, 0.8 m, carved from your views). Not installed yet: it goes in with KM2-1.

Known limits

  • The trees: straight on and from the street each is your front view; at 45 degrees their crowns show flat-sided pads with grey undersides, and the persimmon is a little flat from the side (its side view draws the crown 2 m deep, its front 3.8 m wide). The camera never sees them from below and turns at most about 30 degrees.
  • The pots: no view sees a bonsai pad's underside or a pot's bottom, so those take the nearest view's pixels and come out grey-edged and streaked; at 45 degrees the leaves' edges show faint fringes where two views' cut-outs meet, and the round shrub shows green streaks at the sides of its dome. The game camera never sees the undersides or bottoms, turns at most about 30 degrees, and from the street a pot stands about 55 px tall on a 720-line screen. Kept as they are; say if you want them cleaned up all the same.
  • The little shrine: its views disagree, so each part takes its pixels from where a view draws it. Its top view draws the roof as wide as the base with the ridge running across, where every other view runs it front to back, so the roof takes its tiles from the side view. The base behind the shrine, which the top view draws under that roof, takes the paving in front of it. The side view draws the steps standing out in front of the walls, so the walls take the stones behind them. The back view draws the base a fifth narrower and the shrine a tenth wider, each scaled to fit. Its left side copies its right.
  • The bus stop (station platform) is 7.6 m long at its 3.2 m height: your views' proportions, where the prompt said 10 m. Its back and three-quarter views draw the back wall down to the floor; it follows your front and side instead, so from behind you see the benches under the band, coloured from the front. The floor and the roof's underside, which no view shows straight, take the front's concrete and its view of the underside.
  • The house's balcony railing is a 10 cm plate with the window behind it painted on.
  • The tram's roof units: its BACK draws them where its FRONT and TOP have them, not mirrored, so their far faces take the roof's pixels. Only the far side shows it.
  • The scooter: no view shows its nose, so the nose takes its sides' colours (the leg shield white, the basket's mesh across its front, the tyre black); the faces its rear view cannot see do the same. It is one piece: KM1-1 parks it (its side views draw the fork, shock, exhaust and chain case over its wheels, so wheels that roll would carry them round; a riding scooter would need them modelled apart).
  • The ticket gate: its flap is a thin plate set by hand, and its top takes the nearest sides' pixels; its views disagree about the flap.
  • Sides with no view of their own take the opposite side's pixels: every model's left side takes its right side's.
  • Thin parts (poles' bolts and cables, the bicycle's spokes and basket mesh) are in the texture, not the solid; the bicycle's spokes, the gantry's lattice and the bus stop's end rails are cut out.
