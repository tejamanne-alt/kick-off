Kamakura street props

Step 2, KM1-1's street props, rebuilt in high definition from your own views of each (front, right side, back, top and three-quarter, made in ChatGPT). Each prop is carved as one solid from its views' outlines, then textured from the views themselves: every point takes its pixels from the view that faces it squarely and can see it, so nothing takes the pixels of a part in front of it. Heights are the real ones; widths and depths come from your views' proportions.

Each is 1,452 to 1,454 triangles, one closed shell, in its own 1024 texture, the faces toward the street given twice the texels. The board below puts each beside your views: your front view and the model straight on at the same scale, your three-quarter view and the model at three-quarters, then the model from the street.

Fixed in this pass

  • Found and fixed while reviewing (D25): the pole came out spiky (its step bolts and cables, a few voxels thin, collapsed when the mesh was cut to its budget), so parts under 5 to 8 cm now stay in the texture only. The torii's pillars and the pole's column came out square, so posts are rounded in section where the top view cannot see them. The pole, first carved without its top view, had grey slabs where its crossarms cross; it now uses its top where that agrees with its front and side. Grey background showed on the barrier's and the vending machine's backs: a back view drawn 5 to 7 % wider than the front had moved its box, so each view is now lined up by its whole outline, not its box.

Known limits

  • Sides with no view of their own take the opposite side's pixels: every prop's left side takes its right side's. The bicycle's far side takes its near side's, because its BACK view shows its rear end. The prompt now asks for BACK as the side away from the street, and for the same pose in every view.
  • The barrier's top view shows its arm lowered. The arm is raised in the others, so the arm's shape comes from those.
  • Thin parts (the pole's bolts and cables, the bicycle's spokes and basket mesh) are in the texture, not the solid. The bicycle's spokes and basket are cut out of its texture, so you see through them.
  • The crossing signal's guard frame is solid, not hollow: from the front and the side its far bars show through its near ones, and from above the crossbuck and lamps cover its inside, so no view shows the hollow. Its stripes are on every face. A top view without the parts above it would give the hollow.
  • Hollows the views cannot see into are filled: between the bicycle's pedals and its frame, inside the signal's lamp hoods.
