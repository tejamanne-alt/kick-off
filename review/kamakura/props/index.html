Kamakura street props

KM1-1's street props, buildings, set pieces and the tram, each built in high definition from your own views of it (front, right side, back, top and three-quarter, made in ChatGPT). A prop is carved as one solid from its views' outlines. A building or set piece (the house, the shrine, the platform) is modelled from simple solids measured in metres off your views: walls, roofs, steps, posts and rails. Either way it is then textured from the views themselves: every point takes its pixels from the view that faces it squarely, can see it and shows the object there. Heights or widths are the real ones from the prompts; the rest comes from your views' proportions.

New in this round: the house, the shrine and the platform remodelled after your note that their three-quarter views were distorted, and the wire gantry from its new views. The other eighteen are as before. A prop is about 1,450 triangles in a 1024 texture; the house 4,850, the shrine and the platform 3,880 each, the gantry 2,910 and the tram 3,878, each in a 2048 texture. The board puts each beside your views: your front view and the model straight on at the same scale, your three-quarter view and the model at three-quarters, then the model from the street.

Fixed in this pass

  • Fixed this round (D28): the house, the shrine and the platform were distorted at three-quarters. Carving from outlines lets anything one view shows standing out run through the whole depth of the others: the house's balcony, door canopy and hoods became bands, the shrine's lantern came out twice and its base folded, and the platform was filled solid up to its back wall. They are now built from flat solids measured off your views, so their faces cannot warp. The platform is an open shelter: posts, a back wall, see-through end rails and a sloping roof.
  • Earlier rounds (D25 to D27): joining halves where their objects overlap, every view of one object the same size, a view colouring only what it shows, the pole's spikes and grey slabs, square posts, grey on the backs, open edges, the red machine's notch, the air conditioner's shelf and the signal's guard frame.

Known limits

  • The little shrine: its views disagree, so each part takes its pixels from where a view draws it. Its top view draws the roof as wide as the base with the ridge running across, where every other view runs it front to back, so the roof takes its tiles from the side view. The base behind the shrine, which the top view draws under that roof, takes the paving in front of it. The side view draws the steps standing out in front of the walls, so the walls take the stones behind them. The back view draws the base a fifth narrower and the shrine a tenth wider, each scaled to fit. Its left side copies its right.
  • The station platform is 7.6 m long at its 3.2 m height: your views' proportions, where the prompt said 10 m. The bench is painted on the front railing, since your front view shows it only through the bars. The floor and the roof's underside, which no view shows straight on, take the front view's concrete and its view of the underside.
  • The house's balcony railing is a 10 cm plate with the window behind it painted on.
  • The tram's roof units: its BACK draws them where its FRONT and TOP have them, not mirrored, so their far faces take the roof's pixels. Only the far side shows it.
  • The ticket gate: its flap is a thin plate set by hand, and its top takes the nearest sides' pixels; its views disagree about the flap.
  • Sides with no view of their own take the opposite side's pixels: every model's left side takes its right side's.
  • Thin parts (poles' bolts and cables, the bicycle's spokes and basket mesh) are in the texture, not the solid; the bicycle's spokes, the gantry's lattice and the platform's end rails are cut out.
